# Social housing (HLM) and the TFPB: current law, compensation, magnitudes and the policy debate

Research note for LVTShift-FR. Date of research: 3 November 2026. Law stated as in force for the 2025 and 2026 tax years, with pending changes flagged.

---

## 0. Headline findings

1. **Duration rule (CGI art. 1384 A, version in force since 1 April 2023, checked on Légifrance).** New social rental dwellings financed more than 50 % by PLAI, PLUS or PLS are exempt for **15 years**, counted **from the year after completion**. The period becomes **25 years** if the subsidy or loan decision falls between **1 July 2004 and 31 December 2026**. It becomes **20 years** (15 + 5) with a certified environmental-performance construction, and **30 years** when both extensions apply.
2. **Acquisition-amélioration is covered, but by art. 1384 C, not 1384 A.** It gets the same 15/25 years, counted from the year after **acquisition**, with a decision window of 1 July 2004 to 31 December 2026. A dwelling that has already had a long exemption cannot get a second one.
3. **In 2025, almost every conventionné PLAI/PLUS/PLS dwelling brought into the social stock from about 2007 onwards is still exempt, and almost everything before about 2005 is taxable.** A 25-year exemption that started in 2006 or later cannot have ended by 2025. A 15-year exemption for a pre-July-2004 decision ended by 2020–2021. Only completions in 2005–2006 are genuinely ambiguous. Section 7 has an implementable rule.
4. **Communes are barely compensated.** Two mechanisms apply: a 1992 rule that compensates only losses above 10 % of the commune's TFPB yield, and post-2005 compensations that have been cut by a cumulative "taux de minoration" since 2009. Measured compensation rates run from 2 % to 12 %, depending on scope and source.
   - Full compensation exists only for dwellings with a financing decision between 1 January 2021 and 30 June 2026, and only for their first 10 years (LF 2022, art. 177). That window has closed and has not been extended.
   - The draft budget for 2027 (PLF 2027, art. 8) extends the 25-year exemption window and the partial, minorée compensation to 2030. It does **not** extend the full art. 177 compensation. This is my reading of the bill text.
5. **The REI "montant réel" excludes exempt and abated bases.** The commune "base nette" (E11) is defined after exemptions and abatements. Compensations are recorded separately (e.g. WC14, WC12QV, WCOMFB). Using REI produit as "what is paid today" is therefore correct from the commune's side. From the landlord's side it slightly overstates net payments, because State-funded dégrèvements (art. 1391 E and 1391 C) are refunded to HLM after the rolls are issued.
6. **QPV abatement (art. 1388 bis).** HLM and SEM dwellings in a priority neighbourhood (QPV) get 30 % off the TFPB base for the tax years **2025 to 2030**. The landlord must sign the contrat de ville and an annexed convention with the commune, the EPCI and the préfet. The QPV is defined by art. 5 of loi 2014-173, i.e. the list in force: the 2024 list for metropolitan France. The State compensates 40 %.
7. **The TFPB is not a recoverable charge.** The HLM list in décret 82-955 (annex, heading VIII) allows only the droit de bail, the TEOM (taxe ou redevance d'enlèvement des ordures ménagères) and the taxe de balayage. Rents of conventionné dwellings can rise by at most the IRL each year (CCH L. 353-9-3). The TEOM is due even on exempt dwellings (CGI 1521 I) and is passed on to tenants.
8. **Orders of magnitude.**
   - Foregone TFPB on exempt social housing: €0.43–0.72 bn a year in 2015–2019, depending on scope. Plausibly about €0.8–1.0 bn today (my inference; there is no official figure after 2019).
   - QPV abatement: about €0.32 bn of foregone TFPB in 2024, of which about €0.13 bn is compensated (my computation from the REI 2024).
   - HLM pay about €2.5 bn of TFPB a year, more than 10 % of their rents (USH, 2022; seen only in snippets).
   - About **20–25 % of the 5.4 million social dwellings** are probably under exemption in 2025 (my estimate from RPLS 2025).
9. **Political-economy warning.** Ending the exemptions **and** the abatement removes about €1.1–1.3 bn a year of relief from social landlords, before any land-value redistribution. That is the same order as the RLS (réduction de loyer de solidarité: €1.1 bn in 2025, €0.9 bn proposed for 2027), which was the most contested housing measure of the past decade. The site text must face this directly.

## 2. Question 1: the exemption for new social rental housing (CGI art. 1384 A, with 1384 B, C, C bis and D)

### 2.1 Scope (1384 A I, second paragraph; BOFiP BOI-IF-TFB-10-90-10)
A dwelling qualifies if all four conditions hold:
- it is a **logement locatif social** within the meaning of 278 sexies I 1°, i.e. covered by an APL convention (CCH L. 831-1 3° or 5°);
- it is **new**: a new construction, reconstruction or addition;
- it is the occupant's **main residence**;
- it is financed **more than 50 % by a "prêt réglementé"** (278 sexies I 2°). The threshold falls to 30 % for usufruct/bare-ownership splits ("démembrement").

What counts as a prêt réglementé (CCH D. 331-1; BOFiP §190):
- **PLAI and PLUS** (Caisse des Dépôts, possibly with a State subsidy);
- **PLS** (CDC or approved banks);
- older equivalents: PLA, PLA-CFF, PLC-CFF;
- overseas: LLS, LLTS and PLS under CCH R. 372-1.

Counting the 50 %: for PLAI and PLUS (and LLS/LLTS overseas) operations, subsidies from the State, ANRU, local authorities and Action Logement (PEEC) are added to the loan. For **PLS they are not** (BOFiP §260).

Exclusions:
- old buildings bought with aided loans, even after heavy works; these fall under 1384 C (BOFiP §70);
- **PLI** and non-conventionné dwellings, because they are not "logements locatifs sociaux";
- under **art. 1384 G** (BOFiP §60), new dwellings that replace demolished social dwellings which had already been exempt, in communes where social housing is a majority. I saw this rule in BOFiP but did not open art. 1384 G itself.

**VEFA** (off-plan purchase) is covered: BOFiP §510 deals with a builder selling to a social landlord before letting.

### 2.2 Durations, by date of the subsidy or loan decision

| Decision date (subsidy or aided loan) | Standard | With certified energy/environmental performance (I bis) |
|---|---|---|
| Before 1 July 2004 | **15 years** | **20 years** (chantier opened from 1 Jan 2002) |
| 1 July 2004 to 31 December 2026 (I ter) | **25 years** | **30 years** |
| From 1 January 2027 (current law) | 15 years | 20 years |
| From 1 January 2027 if PLF 2027 art. 8 is adopted | 25 years until 31 Dec 2030 | 30 years until 31 Dec 2030 |

How the window moved:
- the original 2004 window ended in 2009;
- it was extended to 31 Dec 2014, then to 31 Dec 2018 (BOFiP 2012 and 2015 versions);
- then to 31 Dec 2022 (BOFiP 2022 version);
- then to 31 Dec 2026 by LF 2023 (loi 2022-1726, art. 65; current Légifrance text).

Each extension kept the window continuous from 1 July 2004. The Senate adopted extensions to 2028 during the PLF 2026 debate (I-1755, I-2374), but they are **not** in the final LF 2026 (loi n° 2026-103 du 19 février 2026): the Légifrance text in force still says 2026.

Nuance on the 30-year combination: the 2015 BOFiP table applied it only to chantiers opened from 16 July 2006, after the ENL law. The current BOFiP no longer mentions that date. It makes no difference for 2025.

The HQE extension needs a departmental certificate attached to the art. 1406 declaration.

### 2.3 When the clock starts
- **1384 A: "à compter de l'année qui suit celle de leur achèvement"** (statute and BOFiP §20). A dwelling completed in year N is exempt from N+1 to N+D.
- The exemption depends on filing the completion declaration **within 90 days** (CGI 1406 I). If it is filed late, the exemption runs only for the remaining period after 31 December of the year following the filing (1406 II; BOFiP §300–320). Some eligible dwellings may therefore have lost part of their exemption.
- The exemption also covers the TSE, GEMAPI and TASA, **but not the TEOM** (1521 I; BOFiP §5–10).
- **Clawback** (BOFiP §83–90): the exemption ends from 1 January of the following year, for good, if the APL convention is terminated, the use changes or the loan is called in. A **sale to another landlord keeps the exemption** for the remaining period, because the convention follows the dwelling (CCH L. 353-4).
- **2021 TFPB reform**: the departmental TFPB share moved to the communes. Former departmental prolongations under 1586 A became partial exemptions of the communal share (art. 1382-0; BOFiP §II). The exemption now bites on the merged communal rate.

### 2.4 Acquisition-amélioration and acquisitions (art. 1384 C)
- Dwellings **acquired for letting with State financial support** (L. 831-1 3° and 5°, i.e. PLAI, PLUS or PLS acquisitions, with or without works) are exempt for **15 years from the year after acquisition**. The period is **25 years** if the subsidy or loan decision falls between **1 July 2004 and 31 Dec 2026**.
- **No second exemption**: 1384 C does not apply to dwellings that already benefited from 1384, 1384 A, 1384 B, 1384 C or 1384 F.
- The period falls back to **15 years** for dwellings bought **from another HLM body with a PLS**.
- Dwellings improved with ANAH aid by approved non-profit bodies (intermédiation locative) are exempt for 15 years from the year after the works (25 years if the ANAH decision falls between 1 July 2004 and 2026). The subsidy decision must come within two years.
- **Opt-out (1384 C III)**: communes and EPCI where social housing (in the SRU sense) is **at least 50 % of main residences** may abolish the 1384 C exemption for their share, for acquisitions after their deliberation. 1384 B has the same option.

### 2.5 Other social housing exemptions in the same block
- **1384 C bis, "seconde vie"** (created by LF 2024): heavy renovation of social dwellings more than 40 years old, from DPE class E, F or G to class A or B, with agrément from 1 Jan 2024. The exemption lasts **15 years** from the year after the works, or **25 years if the agrément request is filed between 1 Jan 2024 and 31 Dec 2026**. It is compensated by a prélèvement sur recettes (government answer to QE 10965). It has almost no effect in 2025, because the works finish later.
- **1384 D**: temporary or emergency accommodation under a convention with the State. 15 years, or 25 years for decisions between 1 July 2005 and 31 Dec 2026.
- **1384 B**: dwellings under a bail à réhabilitation since 2005, exempt for the length of the lease and "compensées intégralement" by statute. The REI shows the cost is tiny (€4 m of base).
- **1384 E and 1384 F**: dwellings acquired by an EPF in degraded condominiums, and offices converted to housing (on deliberation, 5 years). Marginal for HLM.
- **1384-0 A**: intermediate rental housing (LLI) completed before 1 Jan 2023, up to 20 years. It concerns institutional LLI investors, including HLM-group subsidiaries; it is not HLM stock as such. Seen in the Légifrance search result and CPO RP1; text not opened.

### 2.6 Can open data estimate who is still exempt in 2025?
Yes, with a known error band. See the decision table in section 7.

RPLS (dwelling-level file, SDES) contains:
- CONSTRUCT (completion year), LOCAT (first letting year), PATRIMOINE (year of entry into the landlord's stock);
- ORIGINE: 1 = built, 2 = acquired with works, 3 = acquired without works, 4 = VEFA;
- FINAN and the CUS category: 10/11 PLAI-type, 12 PLA, 13 PLUS, 14 PLS, 16 PLI, 50–55 and 99 old financing before 1977;
- CONV (conventionné or not) and DATCONV;
- QPV_CODE (declared by the landlord) and the geocoded zone (PLG_ZONE, including QP24).

RPLS has **no subsidy or loan decision date**. The rule therefore uses the completion or first-letting year as a proxy, and the threshold sits where a pre-July-2004 decision becomes implausible.

The definitive source would be the MAJIC-based Fichiers fonciers (Cerema). From memory, their exemption records carry the exemption nature and its start and end years per dwelling, in a table like "pb30_pevexoneration". **Field names not verified**, and access requires a convention.

## 3. Question 2: how the State compensates the communes

### 3.1 The layers (CGCT L. 2335-3, R. 2335-4; LF 2022 art. 177)
1. **Base rule (R. 2335-4, 1992 decree, current wording 2015).** It covers 1384, 1384-0 A, 1384 A, 1384 C, 1384 D and 1385 I and II bis. The commune receives an allocation only if its loss **exceeds 10 % of its total TFPB yield**, and only for the excess over that 10 %. Most communes get nothing.
2. **Extension from 15 to 25 years (L. 2335-3, second paragraph).** The loss is compensated, but multiplied since 2009 by a **taux de minoration**.
3. **First 15 or 20 years for decisions between 1 December 2005 and 31 December 2026 (L. 2335-3, third paragraph), except PLS new builds.** Compensated by a prélèvement sur recettes, again multiplied since 2009 by the minoration rate. The rate was cut year after year from 2009 to 2017 (LF 2009 to LF 2017), and the cumulative rate has been frozen since 2018 (2022 for some lines).
4. **LF 2022, art. 177 (from the Rebsamen commission).** The State tops up to **100 % of the loss during the first 10 years of exemption** for communes, EPCI and the Métropole de Lyon, after layers 1–3. It applies to dwellings whose financing decision (State, delegated authority, ANRU, PSLA, Action Logement for Foncière Logement) falls **between 1 Jan 2021 and 30 June 2026**. Years 11 to 25 fall back to layers 1–3. A government evaluation report was due by 30 Sept 2024 (II); not found.
5. **Since LF 2024**, the 1384 C bis "seconde vie" exemption is compensated by a prélèvement sur recettes (government answer, 23 April 2024).

### 3.2 Recent and pending changes
- **The art. 177 window closed on 30 June 2026.** It has not been extended (Légifrance shows no amendment; deputies asked the question in QE 17131 and 17253 in July 2026, with no answer yet). Operations approved before that date keep full compensation for their first 10 years.
- **PLF 2027, art. 8 (bill n° 3210, 1 Oct 2026).**
  - I 2° replaces "2026" with "2030" in 1384 A I ter, 1384 C I, 1384 C bis II and 1384 D.
  - II replaces "2026" with "2030" in the third paragraph of L. 2335-3 (and of L. 5214-23-2, L. 5215-35 and L. 5216-8-1 for groupements).
  - It does **not** touch LF 2022 art. 177. My reading is that the bill extends the minorée compensation, not full compensation. Press reports that the compensation "is extended" (hlm.coop, Gazette) should be read in that light.
- According to hlm.coop (12 Oct 2026), the AN finance committee adopted an amendment that would replace full exemption for operations financed from 2027 with a declining schedule: 75 % in years 1–5, 50 % in years 6–10, 25 % in years 11–15. The committee then rejected part one of the bill, so the floor debated the government text. **Final outcome unknown at 3 Nov 2026.** The housing minister told the Senate on 28 Oct 2026 that the exemption is extended to 2030 (Localtis, 29 Oct 2026).

### 3.3 Does the REI produit exclude exempt bases, and are compensations separate?
**Yes to both** (REI 2024 variable definitions, read in TRACE_REI_2024.xlsx):
- **E11 "FB – COMMUNE / BASE NETTE"**: base after the abatements and exemptions that apply at commune level. E31 is the same for the EPCI.
- **E13 "FB – COMMUNE / MONTANT RÉEL"**: the sum of TFPB cotisations issued for the commune (base × rate + lissage, rounding). E33 is the EPCI equivalent.
- **Compensations are separate variables**:
  - **WC14** "allocation compensatrice – exonérations de longue durée" (commune) and **WE14** (EPCI);
  - **WC12QV** and **WE12QV** for the 1388 bis abatement;
  - **WCOMFB** and **WEPCIFB** for the sum of all FB allocations.
  - EPCI values are **repeated on every member commune**, so de-duplicate them by SIREPCI.
- The **1388 bis abated base** is published (**G211** commune, **G213** EPCI). The 1384 A exempt base is **not** in the REI. It appears in the état fiscal 1387 TF sent to each local authority, which I did not see.
- **Caveat for "what HLM pay".** State-funded dégrèvements (1391 E energy works, 1391 C accessibility works) are paid by the State. The commune still receives the full roll amount, so REI E13 includes TFPB that the HLM later recovers. The order of magnitude is about €115 m a year nationally (€52 m energy + €62 m accessibility in 2017–2019; Sénat rapport général PLF 2019). That is about 4–5 % of HLM TFPB.
- I could not confirm whether the **art. 177 full compensation** is booked inside WC14 or as a separate prélèvement sur recettes. This needs checking before using WC14 as "compensation received".

## 4. Question 3: the 30 % abatement in priority neighbourhoods (CGI art. 1388 bis; version since 31 Dec 2023, LF 2024 art. 73)

- **Who**: rental dwellings (CCH L. 441-1) owned by an **HLM body (L. 411-2) or an SEM**, that have had an exemption under 1384, 1384 A or 1385 II bis, or were acquired before 1 Jan 1998 with State aid. In practice this covers almost all conventionné HLM stock. The abatement matters for dwellings whose long exemption has ended, since an exempt dwelling has no taxable base.
- **Where**: in a **QPV as defined by art. 5 of loi 2014-173**, i.e. the list in force. For metropolitan France that is the list in **décret n° 2023-1314** (in force 1 Jan 2024, 1,362 QPV; snippet-level). The overseas list applies from 2025.
- **Conditions**:
  - at 1 January of the tax year, the landlord has signed the **contrat de ville** and a **convention annexed to it with the commune, the EPCI and the préfet** on maintenance and management of the stock (better service to tenants);
  - the landlord files a declaration before 1 January of the first year;
  - each year, the landlord reports on the actions funded by the abatement to the signatories and the conseil citoyen.
- **Period**: **tax years 2025 to 2030**. Derogations:
  - for 2025 in metropolitan France, the convention and declaration could be filed up to 31 March 2025 (LF 2025 art. 114);
  - for 2026 overseas, up to 31 March 2026 (LF 2026 art. 20).
  The previous regime covered 2016–2022, then 2023–2024.
- **Compensation**: **40 %** of the loss (ecologie.gouv.fr). REI 2024 is consistent: WC12QV of €120 m set against a computed communal cost of €301 m.
- **2024 order of magnitude (my computation from REI 2024)**:
  - abated base: €722.5 m communal (693 communes) and €426 m intercommunal;
  - foregone TFPB: about €301 m (communes) + €15 m (EPCI) = **about €316 m**;
  - compensation: about €120 m + €6 m = **about €126 m**.
- **Nuance (CPO RP4)**: the abatement is a counterpart for extra management effort in QPV ("gestion urbaine de proximité"). It is the one HLM relief with an explicit, audited obligation in return.

## 5. Question 4: other reliefs, and whether TFPB can be recovered from tenants

- **1391 E, dégrèvement for energy-saving works**: 25 % of the works paid in the previous year (net of subsidies) is deducted from the TFPB. HLM, energy-related SEM and L. 365-1 bodies are eligible. It does not stack with 1384 C bis. Energy-savings certificates (CEE) count as subsidies after a Conseil d'État decision of 14 June 2022 (secondary source). Cost about €52 m in 2017–2019, borne by the State.
- **1391 C, accessibility and adaptation works for disabled people**: deductible from the TFPB paid to communes. Cost about €62 m in 2017–2019, borne by the State and rising (Cour des comptes 2023).
- **Permanent exemptions** (1382) do not cover HLM rental stock. **Generic temporary exemptions**: the 2-year new-build exemption (1383) is superseded by 1384 A for social housing.
- **Recoverability**:
  - **TFPB: not recoverable.** The limitative HLM list (CCH L. 442-3; décret 82-955, annex VIII "Impositions et redevances") includes only the **droit de bail, the TEOM (taxe or redevance) and the taxe de balayage**.
  - **TEOM: recoverable**, and still due on exempt dwellings (CGI 1521 I).
  - **Rents**: conventionné rents are revised once a year, **within the IRL change** (CCH L. 353-9-3), and capped by the convention maximum.
  - So the **legal and short-run incidence of the TFPB is on the landlord**. Over the long run the cost can show up as less construction or renovation, or as rents pushed towards the legal caps when dwellings are relet. The site text should say so honestly (see section 9).

## 6. Question 5: orders of magnitude

### 6.1 Cost of the exemptions and compensation

| Year | Foregone TFPB, social housing exemptions | State compensation | Rate | Source and scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | n.a. | n.a. | about 6 % (communes and groupements about 9 %, departments 0 %) | Cour des comptes référé 2017 |
| 2015 | **€0.7 bn** (about 95 % borne by local authorities) | about €35 m implied | about 5–6 % | Cour référé 2017 (all levels, incl. departments) |
| 2017 | €425.8 m | €13.9 m | about 3 % | Sénat rapport n° 731 (Dallier, 2019) |
| 2018 | €500.4 m | €16.2 m | 3.2 % | Government report to Parliament, Nov 2019 (via Localtis). Full compensation estimated at €484 m |
| 2019 | €715 m | €86 m | 12 % | AMF figures, cited by Cour des comptes 2023 |
| 2021 | n.a. | €23 m (State-borne part) | n.a. | CPO RP3, 2023 |
| 2022 | n.a. | €41 m (State cost) | n.a. | CPO 2023, n. 91 (the RN amendment I-1180 cites €38.8 m from a ministerial answer) |
| 2024 | n.a. | **€52.7 m** long-duration allocations (communes €50.1 m + EPCI €2.6 m, de-duplicated) | n.a. | My computation, REI 2024 (WC14, WE14) |
| 2025 (estimate) | **about €0.8–1.0 bn** | about €50 m + art. 177 amounts | about 5–10 % | My inference, see below |

Basis for the 2025 estimate:
- the stock is growing;
- since 2021 the bloc communal bears the former departmental rate;
- rental values were revalued by 7.1 % (2023), 3.9 % (2024) and 1.7 % (2025);
- cross-check: about 1.2 m exempt dwellings × about €700 ≈ €0.85 bn;
- partisan estimates in amendments: €0.7–0.9 bn (centrist Sénat, I-2555), "avoisine le milliard" (ecologist Sénat, II-1469), "près d'un milliard" (RN, AN I-1180).

The rates differ because the sources measure different things: some cover only the extension, some all levels of government, some all compensated categories.

Context from the **Cour des comptes 2023**: all temporary TFPB exemptions together cost €2.25 bn in 2021, with a **State compensation rate of about 2 %**, down from 10 % in 2016. The Cour describes these exemptions as poorly tracked, poorly costed and only partly compensated.

**Tax-expenditure accounting**: these exemptions reduce **local** taxes, so their cost **is not counted in the Voies et moyens tome II**. Only the State compensations show up (CPO RP3; Cour référé 2017 n. 1). CPO proposal n° 4 (2023) is to list TFPB exemptions and dégrèvements in an annex to Voies et moyens tome II.

### 6.2 Share of the social stock under exemption (RPLS at 1 Jan 2025, national totals I extracted from the SDES workbook)
- **Stock**: 5,396,259 dwellings; **1,671,035 in QPV (31 %)**.
- **By original financing**: PLAI 410,854; PLUS after 1977 2,244,910; PLUS before 1977 2,187,177; PLS 385,627; PLI 167,691.
- **By age of construction**: under 5 years 313,670; 5–10 years 397,065; 10–20 years 665,943. That is **1.38 m built in the last 20 years (25.5 %)**.
- **Flow of new social dwellings built** (SDES series): 1.08 m between 2007 and 2024, i.e. **20 %** of today's stock. On top of that come about 6–9 k acquisitions a year (2024: 3.7 k with works, 2.0 k without; VEFA 31.0 k and own construction 34.6 k).
- **Estimate**: about **20–25 % of the social stock (about 1.1–1.35 m dwellings) is exempt in 2025**. Netting out PLI, non-conventionné stock and pre-2004 decisions completed in 2005–2006 pushes the figure down; adding acquisitions since 2005 pushes it up. This is my estimate, not an official figure.

### 6.3 HLM TFPB actually paid
About **€2.5 bn a year, more than 10 % of rents**, the third-largest cost item (USH Repères n° 101, 2022; snippet only). A much older parliamentary answer gave 10–11 points of rent for an average landlord.

Total relief from TFPB rules on social housing today: exemptions about €0.8–1.0 bn, QPV abatement about €0.32 bn, State dégrèvements about €0.1 bn. That is **about €1.2–1.4 bn**, roughly half of what HLM pay and about 5 % of rents (my calculation).

## 8. Question 6: the policy debate, critiques and counter-arguments

### 8.1 Critiques of the HLM TFPB exemptions (sourced)

1. **Poor targeting, set by the local tax rate rather than by need.** The exemption is worth more where the local TFPB rate is high, regardless of how much new social housing is needed (Cour des comptes, référé S2017-1737, 2017; repeated by the CPO in 2023). The Cour headed its section *« des mesures mal ciblées »* (exemptions from both corporate tax and TFPB).
2. **The cost falls hardest on the communes that host the most social housing.** In the 113 metropolitan cities over 50,000 inhabitants, the revenue loss rose from about 1 % to about 5 % of TFPB as the social housing share went from 15 % to 35 %. The communes most penalised are often those where the need for funding is greatest (Cour référé 2017). The CPO (2023) notes a positive correlation between the size of the relief and the number of social dwellings, which penalises the areas of greatest need.
3. **A hidden, unbudgeted subsidy.** State budget documents show only the small compensations; most of the cost is invisible (Cour référé 2017, n. 1). Because the cost falls on local authorities, it is **not counted in Voies et moyens** (CPO RP3, 2023, proposal n° 4). The Cour (2023) calls TFPB exemptions poorly tracked, poorly costed and only partly compensated.
4. **Compensation is minimal and has been cut on purpose since 2009.** Compensation counts as a "variable d'ajustement" and has been minorée every year. Measured rates: 2–12 % (section 6.1). Full compensation (art. 177) was temporary, covered only 10 of 25 years, and its window has closed.
5. **It discourages mayors from building social housing, and so affects where it gets built.** The Rebsamen commission (2021), as summarised in the government's own answer to QE 10965 (JO 23 April 2024), found that the exemptions' weight on communal budgets, while new residents add costs, is one of the main disincentives for mayors. The Cour (2023) adds that since the abolition of the taxe d'habitation, communes no longer have any tax incentive to build social housing.
6. **Better replaced by targeted, budgeted support.** **Recommendation n° 2 of the Cour (2017)**: replace the TFPB exemption regime for social housing with **subsidies targeted to the situation of the territories and landlords concerned**. The CPO (2023, RP1) recalls the recommendation. The Cour also noted HLM reserves (equity of €161 bn against €130 bn of debt in 2014) and judged the sector able to absorb a €1 bn cut in tax advantages.
7. **Partial opt-outs already exist.** Since LF 2017, majority-social communes may abolish the 1384 C (and 1384 B) exemptions, and 1384 G excludes some reconstructions. Parliament has thus already acknowledged the concentration problem.

### 8.2 Counter-arguments (to acknowledge fairly)

1. **It is central to the financing of new operations, especially very social ones.** The ministry, replying to the Cour in 2017, said the exemption plays a decisive role in the financial balance of HLM construction, especially for programmes with very low rents. It opposed converting it into subsidies, given the State's budget constraints and the need for guarantees to landlords. The USH-backed amendments (2025) call it one of the main aids to producing social housing, and say it lowers rents for the whole life of the dwelling.
2. **A tax exemption is a 25-year commitment; a subsidy can be cut in any annual budget.** The RLS since 2018 (€1.3 bn a year at its peak, €1.1 bn in 2025, €0.9 bn proposed for 2027) and the volatility of the State's contribution to the FNAP (aides à la pierre) show the risk. Landlords' business plans assume the exemption.
3. **Landlords' finances are already under strain.** Pressures include the RLS, the rise in the Livret A rate (a cost of about €3.5 bn according to the USH, cited by CPO RP4), and renovation needs (about €9 bn a year according to the USH; €7–8 bn according to the SGPE, cited by CPO RP4). TFPB already costs HLM about €2.5 bn, more than 10 % of rents.
4. **Construction has slowed.** Every recent budget has extended the regime (LF 2023 to 2026; PLF 2027 to 2030). The Senate voted extensions across the spectrum (SER and centrists, PLF 2026). Ending the exemptions in a downturn would be criticised as making the shortage worse.
5. **Social landlords cannot pass the tax on directly to tenants** (not a recoverable charge, rents capped at IRL). This is the basis for saying the reform falls on landlords. It also means the cost comes out of investment capacity, and over time that harms applicants waiting for housing.
6. **The QPV abatement funds extra local management** under an audited convention. Removing it may cut services in the poorest neighbourhoods unless it is replaced.

Note on sources: some of the sharpest attacks on the exemptions come from the **RN** (AN I-1180, 2025), framed against the "para-public sector", and from right-of-centre deputies (PPL 4353, 2021: limit the exemption to 5 years). For a left-of-centre reader, rely on the **Cour des comptes, the CPO, the Rebsamen commission (a mixed commission chaired by a PS mayor) and the government's own answers**, not on those texts.

## 9. How the site can frame this (draft lines, in French, with their sources)

These are my own draft wordings, for adaptation.

1. « Aujourd'hui, un logement social neuf est exonéré de taxe foncière pendant 25 ans (30 ans s'il est très performant), à compter de l'année qui suit son achèvement. » (CGI art. 1384 A, I ter; BOFiP BOI-IF-TFB-10-90-50)
2. « Cette aide n'apparaît pas dans le budget de l'État : elle est payée par les communes qui accueillent les logements, et l'État ne leur en rembourse qu'une faible part, de 2 % à 12 % selon les années et les sources. » (CGCT L. 2335-3, R. 2335-4; Cour des comptes 2017 and 2023; CPO 2023)
3. « La Cour des comptes a recommandé dès 2017 de remplacer cette exonération par des subventions ciblées, tenant compte des besoins des territoires et des organismes. » (Cour des comptes, référé S2017-1737, recommandation n° 2)
4. « Plus une ville compte de logements sociaux, plus elle perd de recettes : de l'ordre de 1 % à 5 % de sa taxe foncière quand la part de logements sociaux passe de 15 % à 35 %. » (Cour des comptes, référé 2017, villes de plus de 50 000 habitants)
5. « La taxe foncière n'est pas une charge récupérable : c'est le bailleur qui la paie, pas le locataire. Pour qu'elle ne pèse ni sur les loyers ni sur la construction, nous proposons que l'aide au logement social soit versée directement, de façon visible et garantie. » (décret 82-955, annexe VIII; CCH L. 353-9-3; framing ours)

**Advice on the framing.**
- Present the reform as **ending a hidden, unequally spread subsidy and replacing it with an equal or larger, visible and legally guaranteed one**. Do not present it as cutting support for social housing.
- Give the order of magnitude: about €1.2–1.4 bn of TFPB relief, comparable to the RLS.
- Suggest a credible replacement: for example a per-dwelling grant at agrément, or a dedicated, legally earmarked State envelope.
- Acknowledge the main objection plainly: a budget subsidy is easier to cut than a statutory exemption.

## 10. Uncertainties and open points

1. **2027 onwards depends on PLF 2027 art. 8**: the window may be extended to 2030, a declining schedule may be adopted, or the regime may fall back to 15 years. None of this changes the 2025 tax year.
2. **Compensation accounting**: whether the art. 177 full compensation is inside REI WC14 or a separate prélèvement sur recettes; the value of the cumulative minoration coefficient; and why the published compensation rates diverge (3 % vs 12 %), which is mostly a matter of scope.
3. **The 2005–2006 completion cohort**: the decision date is not in RPLS. The cohort is about 70 k dwellings (1.3 % of the stock).
4. **Late declarations** (1406 II), **HQE certificates** and **1384 C III opt-outs** are not in open data.
5. **Inter-landlord transfers** (rule 4): RPLS ORIGINE 3 mixes market acquisitions with stock transfers. The LOCAT/PATRIMOINE test is a heuristic.
6. **QPV abatement coverage**: not every landlord signs a convention in every QPV. Use REI G211/G213 for exact commune totals. The 2024 QPV list decree was seen only in snippets.
7. **Dégrèvements** (1391 E/C): the national figures are from 2017–2019 and there is no commune-level open data.
8. **USH €2.5 bn figure**: snippet only (2022). An up-to-date figure would come from DHUP/Ancols financial statements, which I did not consult.
9. **The 2025 cost estimate (€0.8–1.0 bn)** is an inference. No official national figure has been published since 2019. The CPO's proposal n° 4 would fix this.
10. **No IPP, CAE, IGF or Fondation Abbé Pierre publication** specifically on HLM TFPB exemptions was found, and nothing from Trannoy & Wasmer on the point could be verified. Do not cite them on it.
